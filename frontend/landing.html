The problem

Misinformation spreads fastest
in family group chats.

Forwarded messages bypass every editorial filter. A false claim can reach thousands of people in minutes — in languages most fact-check tools cannot read.

01

Speed beats accuracy

A forward takes one tap to send. Checking each claim manually takes minutes most people will not spend in a group chat context.

02

Regional language gap

Most fact-check tools work in English only. Forwards in Tamil, Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, or Hinglish are effectively unchecked.

03

No shareable correction

Even when a fact-check exists, sharing a long article into a family group rarely works. People need a short, respectful reply they can paste directly.

04

Trust through repetition

The more times a claim is forwarded, the more credible it appears. Speed of correction matters as much as correctness of the correction itself.

How it works

Five steps. One verifiable pipeline.

Every claim goes through the same process. No black boxes — you see the exact queries run, the sources retrieved, and the reasoning behind every verdict.

STEP 01
Claim extraction
LLM detects language, isolates up to three checkable factual claims, and writes a precise English and native-language search query for each.
STEP 02
Live evidence
SerpApi runs three parallel searches per claim — Google Web ("claim fact check"), Google News, and a native-language query — all India-localised (gl=in).
STEP 03
Source ranking
Results are scored by credibility tier. Indian fact-checkers, .gov.in, PIB, RBI, and WHO rank above general news and other web results.
STEP 04
Evidence verdict
The LLM judges each claim using only the retrieved search results. It cannot invent facts. Every citation is validated against the source list.
STEP 05
Group reply
A short, factual correction is written in the forward's own language, with source links — ready to paste back into the group immediately.
Verdict system

Four possible outcomes.
No guessing.

Every verdict is grounded strictly in the retrieved evidence. When evidence is absent or conflicting, the system says so — it does not fabricate a conclusion.

False

A credible fact-checker or official source directly contradicts the claim. The specific claim has been actively investigated and debunked.

True

Multiple credible and independent sources clearly support the claim without significant qualification, missing context, or contradiction.

Misleading

The claim has a factual basis but omits critical context, is substantially exaggerated, or refers to a situation that has since changed.

Unverified

Search results are absent, too sparse, or mutually contradictory. The claim cannot be confirmed or ruled out on the available evidence.

Evidence layer

Live search, not training data.

The LLM reasons over evidence — it does not provide it. Every verdict cites current, retrievable, publicly accessible search results.

Google Search

Fact-check queries

Each claim is searched as "claim fact check" with Indian localisation (gl=in). Finds existing debunks from Indian fact-checking organisations and official government clarifications.

Google News

Current reporting

A parallel news search retrieves the latest journalism on each claim. Essential for recent events where training data is stale — new hoaxes appear daily.

Regional search

Native-language results

When the forward is not in English, a third query runs in the original language with the correct Google hl= parameter — surfacing regional fact-checks English queries miss entirely.
